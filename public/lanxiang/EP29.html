Lin Jinqi lays the plan before her on the return visit with the calm of a man who has already decided and merely informs the world. He speaks of the capital as the seat of the new emperor he helped set on the throne, a place where the Jing'an household belongs now that the realm is knit together, and he asks her to come as his wife and not as a prisoner of the decree. Lanxiang hears the courtesy and answers with the same stillness she used at the tea service: she will not go. Her reasons are the ones the season has tended — the parents she was only just allowed to leave, and the old shadow over the city that once turned her true family away. She does not raise her voice; she simply sets her cup down and names the weight, and the man who won her by petition meets, again, the wall of her memory. The season marks how little the marriage has moved her: the bride who slept in the warm chamber still keeps her own door.

The season, which has followed Lü Jier from the night she helped Lanxiang escape the river road, lets the name strike its old chord: the companion who saved the bride once is now the one the bride cannot reach, and the capital that Lanxiang feared becomes, in Azhi's few words, the place where the woman she owes is kept.

Lanxiang, who would not move for a marquis or a throne, feels the ground shift under the name of a friend. Lanxiang wakes not in the small house she loves nor in the Lin residence she endured, but to the slow roll of water under a plank and the smell of river in her hair.
