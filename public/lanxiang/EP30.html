The blow that wins the contracts is struck not with words but with a club, upon a lawn where the capital's ladies pass the idle hour at a gentle game of chuiwan, a ball struck through hoops with a slender stick. Zhao Xingtang, certain of her rank over this upstart bride, challenges her to the game for sport — and Lanxiang, who learned a steady eye in harder schools, answers with a single flawless stroke that sends the ball true through the hoop at the first attempt. The gathered ladies, who came to watch a maid be humbled, instead watch a marquis's wife bend the game to her will, and Zhao Xingtang, stung by the public grace of it, honors the wager she did not mean to lose. The contracts change hands across the grass, and the season marks the strange victory: the girl who was once a ball knocked about the great house now drives one home by her own aim, and the friend she sailed north to free is, at last, free.

Freed, Lü Jier declares the life she means to live: she will go to Quanzhou and register herself as a woman-headed household, a household with no man's name above the door, and make her own way by the sea she has learned to trust. The season notes the quiet echo: the bride who was given away twice now gives, freely, the means for another woman to belong to no one.
