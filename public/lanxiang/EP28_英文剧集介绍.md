# 兰香如故 — The Fragrance of Orchids Remains
## Episode 28 · Told Like A Story

Let me tell you about the twenty-eighth episode, because this is the one where the bride who slapped her groom the night before must now sit among his family as a daughter-in-law, and she does it wearing her own mother's cloth and not a word of warmth, teaching the Lin estate that the emperor's decree made her a marquis's wife but made her no one's subject. The first morning after the wedding is the day of respect, when a new bride serves tea to the elders and is received into the house's order. Lanxiang comes to it dressed not in the finery the Lin residence would have sewn for her but in the plain, honest garments her parents prepared — a quiet declaration that the woman the emperor gave them is still the daughter of the small house, and that no silk of the marquis's can overwrite the cloth her mother's hands put on her. The season marks the small rebellion: a bride who obeys the rite and defies the wardrobe in the same breath.

The tea service is where her coldness speaks loudest. Lanxiang goes through the motions of the cup and the bow, but she does so in a silence that withholds the soft words a new daughter-in-law is meant to offer, and the hall fills with the awkward music of a bride who will not pretend. The old lady at the head of the room receives the chill without comment, and for a moment the rite threatens to sour into a judgment on the girl. It is Senior Lady Lin and the second aunt who step into the silence, the two older women smoothing the awkwardness with small talk and gentle redirects, shielding Lanxiang from the old lady's displeasure the way a hand shields a flame from the wind. The season marks the rescue: the bride who gave no warmth is kept from the old lady's wrath not by her own sweetness but by the household's older women, who read the storm and turn it aside.

奉茶处，是她的冷说得最响。兰香走了奉盏与拜的过场，却以一声扣住那新媳本该给的软话的静走的，满堂满了个不肯装的新妇的不堪之乐。堂头的老夫人，无言受了这寒；有顷，礼险些酸成对姑娘的判。是林母与二婶，踏进这静里，两个年长的妇，以闲谈与轻引，把那不堪抚平，如一只手把焰从风里护住般，把兰香自老夫人的不悦里护下。这季记这救：那没给暖的新妇，免了老夫人的怒，不因她自己的甜，而因府里年长的妇——她们读了那场风暴、把它拨开。

And in the end the old lady accepts the one gift the bride did mean to give. The fish soup that Lanxiang and Gantang prepared together is set before her, made with a willing hand and offered without the false smile the hall expected, and the hardened elder takes it because even she can be won by a bowl honest in its making. The season lingers on this small victory, because it is the only moment of the morning where Lanxiang gives the house something true rather than something commanded — the soup is hers, not the emperor's, and the old lady's acceptance of it is the one bridge the bride's coldness cannot burn. Gantang, standing behind her mistress, shares the quiet credit, the two girls who survived the sealed house now serving the Lin estate a dish that asks nothing but to be eaten. The rite that began in silence ends, for once, on a note the bride actually meant.

Past the tea, Lin Jinqi raises a larger matter before the elders: he means to take the whole family to the capital. The Jing'an Marquis, now a man the new emperor trusts, speaks of removing the household from the old residence to the city where the throne sits, and the proposal lands like a stone in the quiet — a move that would uproot parents, sisters, and the bride who has only just been carried through the gate. The season lingers on the word "capital," because for Lin Jinqi it is the center of the realm he helped save, but for others at the table it is the place of leaving and loss. The elders receive it as they receive most of what the marquis decides — with a nod that is half assent and half the habit of letting the strong have their way — and the question becomes not whether to go but whether the bride, above all, will suffer herself to be taken there.

茶过，林锦岐在长辈前提起一件更大的事：他要举家迁京。靖安侯，如今是新帝信的人，说要把府从旧居移到御座所在的城；这提议如石落静——一道会把父母、姐妹、与刚被抬过门的 bride 都拔根的行。这季在"京"字上流连，因对林锦岐，它是他救下的域的中心；但对席上别人，它是离与失的处。长辈受它，如受这侯大多的决定——一点首，半是应、半是让强者的习惯；而问题成了：不是去不去，而是那 bride，尤其，肯不肯让自己被带去。

Lanxiang will not suffer it, and she says so with the same stillness she used on the wedding night. Her resistance has two roots the season has tended all along: a daughter's love for the parents she was only just allowed to leave, and an old shadow over the capital itself — the memory of a true family refused at its gates, a wrong done to the name she now carries hidden. To go to the city is, for her, to walk toward the place that turned her blood away, and no marquis's title can dress that road in welcome. She does not storm or weep; she simply names the weight, and the household, which expected a bride broken by the decree, meets instead a wife who draws her own line at the capital's wall. The season marks how far the girl has bent and where she will not: she accepted the marriage the throne forced, but she will not gift it the home she still owes her mother and father.

兰香不肯，以她新婚夜用的同一样静说的。她的拒，有两处这季一直养的根：一个女儿对刚被允离的父母的爱，与一个罩在京城本身的旧影——一桩真家在它门前被拒的忆，一桩加在她如今藏着带的那名上的冤。去那城，对她，是走向那转身斥了她血的处；而再贵的侯衔，也穿不暖那条路的迎。她不闹、不泪；她只点出那重；而那本预期一个被旨打碎的新妇的府，迎来的却是一个在自己画出线的妻——以京城的墙为界。这季记这姑娘弯了多远、与她不肯处：她受了御座强的婚，却不肯把还欠父母的那家，赠作它的礼。

The resistance might have hardened the old master against the move had the second daughter not thrown herself upon his mercy. She kneels outside the hall where the matter is decided, a long and public penance, bearing the weight of a sister's unhappiness on her own knees, and she stays there through the hours the elders argue. The season shows the second daughter grown from the girl who was fooled by a handsome traitor into the woman who now shields her family with her own body — a quiet echo of the third daughter's intervention last week. And into this tableau the third daughter returns, heavy with the child she carries and flanked by the Yongchang Marquis, her husband, to plead the cause of the move and of the bride alike. The pregnant wife and the kneeling sister together make an argument no rod can answer, and the old master, worn down by love, at last nods.

So the journey to the capital is granted, and the household turns toward packing, but the episode saves its strangest grace note for Jiugao. The marquis's faithful retainer, faced with the matchmaking his own family would press upon him, chooses a defense no one expects: he dresses in women's clothing and parades through the streets in full and flamboyant view, so that no reputable family would saddle their daughter to a man who wears a skirt in daylight. The season, which has been all coronations and floggings, allows itself a small laugh at the sight — the loyal servant who feared a wife more than a blade, and who beat the match by becoming the very thing a bride-seeking house would flee. Jiugao's gown is the episode's joke and its quiet truth: in a house where marriages are commanded by emperors and refused by mothers, the only free man is the one who makes himself untouchable by his own absurd hand.

The season pauses, before it closes, on the strange new shape of the Lin household, because the move it has just approved will carry every thread we have followed into the city where the tale began. The marquis has his bride by decree and his capital by right; the bride has her parents' cloth and her refusal of the city's welcome; the second daughter has her knee and the third her belly and her title; Jiugao has his gown. What looks like a household falling into line is in fact a knot of small rebellions, each soul keeping one piece of themselves the emperor's command could not reach. The season, which has pressed Lanxiang from the bathwater to the bridal chamber, now loads her onto a road north with her guard still up and her mother's words still warm on her tongue. The capital waits at the end of it, and with it the old wrong the hidden name was born to set right.

So we close the twenty-eighth episode the way a household closes a door — by accepting the soup, granting the move, and laughing at the man in the gown, even as the bride keeps her silence and her own cloth. Lanxiang served the tea cold and won the old lady with a fish broth; Lin Jinqi named the capital and met the wall of his wife's memory; the second daughter knelt and the third returned heavy with child to break the old master's no; Jiugao escaped a match by becoming a spectacle. Episode twenty-nine will put the bride on the road she resisted and show whether the capital receives her as a marquis's wife or as the ghost of the family it once refused. For now, remember only this: the decree made her a Lin, but the cloth her mother sewed and the soup Gantang helped her make are the only things in that house she gave them freely, and the capital, when she reaches it, will meet a woman who came unwilling and unbowed.
