The wedding morning finds Lanxiang in the small house she loves, bidding her parents a farewell she does not want to make. Xu Wanquan and Lady Xue have raised her as their own, and the daughter they send to the Lin estate today is not the bondservant the house remembers but a bride the emperor named — yet to them she is simply the girl they must let go, and the leaving sits heavy on all three. The procession that carries her toward the marquis's gate meets, halfway down the city, another procession coming the other way — Han Liang's, and his is a procession of triumph rather than of bride-price. He does not stop; she does not step down; but the season marks the meeting as the hinge of the episode: the man who loved her freely, crowned by his own merit, and the woman bound to another by the throne's command, crossing in the same hour, each at the height of a fate the other could not share. The drums of his victory and the flutes of her wedding sound in the same air, and neither player hears the other's song.

The bridal chamber is where the withheld blow finally falls. When the veil is lifted from her face, Lanxiang does not smile or lower her eyes as a new wife should; she raises her hand and strikes Lin Jinqi across the cheek, hard and in full view of the chamber, a slap that rings louder than the small jokes the watchers had prepared. The season holds the line — the same phrase, carried from a mother's humiliation to a daughter's defiance, turns in Lanxiang's mouth from a complaint into a verdict. The night settles, and the marriage bed becomes the last field of the bride's quiet war. Lanxiang will not share the master's couch; she withdraws to the warm chamber, the smaller heated room kept for the cold months, and there she means to pass the wedding night alone, a bride by decree and a stranger by choice. The Lin estate, which has never learned to read her, takes the withdrawal for sulking and leaves her to it, but the season knows better: the girl who survived poison and treason and a sealed house is not pouting, she is keeping the one boundary the emperor's command could not cross. Lin Jinqi will not leave her there. Past the hour when the household has turned in, he comes to the warm chamber against the advice of every servant who fears the lady's temper, and without a word he lifts Lanxiang and carries her back to the main bed — the marriage bed the rite meant for them both. She does not struggle loud enough to wake the court; he does not explain. He lays her down as gently as the blow she gave him allows, and then he withdraws, taking the warm chamber for himself, so that the bride sleeps in the place the law made hers and the groom in the place she abandoned. The season marks the strange tenderness of it: the man who forced the marriage will not force the night, and the girl who slapped him is given, by the very hand she struck, the larger bed and the smaller peace. Years before Lanxiang wore the name Xu, the Shen house sought a match with this same family and was refused, and the mother who loved her then spoke those very words at the shutting of that door. The season, which keeps such debts, lets the daughter repay them now with an open hand: the house that once deemed the Shen blood unfit to marry in is the house that kidnapped the daughter into the bond, and the slap is the refusal returned, generations late. Lin Jinqi, who petitioned the throne to win what his family once denied, receives the old refusal upon his own cheek, and the circle the Lin estate started closes at last upon itself.

So we close the twenty-seventh episode the way a wedding closes a door — by carrying the bride over the threshold she did not choose, and letting her slam the smaller one behind her. Lanxiang said her farewell and met Han Liang's triumph on the road; she withheld the bow until a stranger's hand forced it; she slapped the groom with her mother's old words and slept alone until he carried her to the bed and withdrew. The Lin estate got the marriage the emperor commanded and the wife it had not reckoned on, and Lin Jinqi got the bond he petitioned for and the blow that came with it. Episode twenty-eight will follow the new bride into the morning after, where the household expects a daughter-in-law and meets instead a woman who wears the marquis's name the way she wears the red — because the decree put it there, not because her heart did. For now, remember only this: the slap she dealt was her mother's words come home, and the door the Shen family was refused is the door she walked through to haunt it.
