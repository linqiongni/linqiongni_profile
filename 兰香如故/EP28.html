Episode 28 opens the morning after the wedding, and the first job is to meet the elders. A new bride is supposed to dress fine and pay respect, but Lanxiang has other ideas. She puts on the plain, simple clothes her own parents prepared for her, the kind that shout she did not come here to pretend. No fancy jewelry, no bright silks from the Lin family. She wears what her family gave her, and she wears it on purpose. The other women in the house notice right away, because in a great family the clothes are the first thing everyone reads. By dressing this way she tells the room without a word that she is here under force, and she will not play the happy daughter they wanted. It is a small rebellion, but in that house a small one is still seen by everyone. The morning after a forced wedding is never a soft thing, and she means to start it on her own terms rather than theirs. A plain dress on a rich bride is a small sentence, but in that house everyone reads it clearly.

第二十八集从婚后头一个早上开，头一桩事是拜见长辈。新媳妇本该盛装行礼，可兰香另有主意。她穿上自己爹娘给她备的平实衣裳，那种一眼就写着"我不是来装样"的。不戴首饰，不穿林家的鲜亮绸子。她穿自家给的，还是故意的。府里别的女人一眼就看出来，因为大宅里衣裳是头一件人人读的东西。她这么穿，是不用开口就告诉满屋：她是被人逼来的，她不扮你们要的欢喜女儿。这是点小反骨，可在那府里，小的也人人看得见。

The rite calls for serving tea to the elders, and that is where Lanxiang goes quiet for real. She walks up, pours the tea, and hands the cup, but her face is flat and she does not say a word of the sweet lines a bride is meant to say. No smile, no soft words, no calling anyone grandmother or mother the way they expect. The hall goes still, because everyone knows the script and she is not following it. The old ladies wait for the pretty words, and they do not come. She just stands there with the cup, cold and done. It is the kind of silence that speaks louder than any slip of the tongue, and the family feels it like a draft through a closed door. The elders trade glances as the tea cools a little in her hand before anyone steps in to fill the gap. A bride who will not speak her lines leaves the whole hall to guess what she is thinking, and none of them guess anything kind.

Senior Lady Lin sees the trouble before it spreads, and so does Second Aunt. The two step in to smooth the moment over. They laugh a little, say the girl is just shy, say the clothes are a humble choice that shows good breeding, and turn the flat air into something the room can swallow. They cover for Lanxiang without shaming her, which is more than most mothers would do. Second Aunt in particular keeps the talk moving so no one can press the silent bride. It is a small kindness, and Lanxiang feels it. She does not soften, but she lets the two women carry the minute, because fighting them would only hurt the people who are trying to help her stand in this hard house. For one strange minute the two older women and the silent bride stand on the same side, and the house does not know what to make of it. Kindness from the very people who run the place is the last thing Lanxiang expected on her first morning.

Then comes the part that softens the old lady for real. Lanxiang and Gantang have prepared a fish soup, a plain dish made with care, and they offer it to the eldest grandmother of the house. It is not a grand gift, just honest food from a girl who will not bow but will still cook. The old lady hesitates. Once the tea is done, Lin Jinqi brings up a bigger plan. He tells the elders he wants to take the whole family to the capital. Not just himself, but the old ladies, the sisters, the household, everyone. The capital is where the real power and the old family ties live, and he thinks it is time to move the center there. The proposal lands heavy, because leaving the home they know is no small thing. The elders look at each other, and the room turns from the morning's small drama to a large question. Where they live next will change every life in that house, and Lin Jinqi has just put the idea on the table without warning. Some of the younger sisters brighten at the idea of the city, while the older ones sit with the heavy thought of leaving the only home they have known. The capital is a far word, and Lin Jinqi has dropped it into the room like a stone into still water.

Lanxiang hears the plan and pushes back hard. She does not want to go. The reason is plain to her: she misses her own parents already, and the thought of leaving them farther behind hurts. On top of that, the capital holds a bad memory for her. It is where her own family, the Shen family, fell apart years ago, and the shadow of that old ruin still sits on the city in her mind. She will not say yes to walking back into the place that broke her blood. So she says no, quietly but firmly, and the house sees that the new bride has a line she will hold even against her husband. The capital is not just a city to her. It is the scene of a wound. She does not raise her voice, but her no is clear, and the capital plan meets its first real wall in the girl who was just married into the family. The city that broke the Shen name is the last place she will walk into willing, and she says so without shame.

The move is not decided yet, because one of the sisters blocks it in her own way. It is a quiet protest, a girl using her body to say no when her voice is not enough. The family does not know what to do with a daughter planted on the floor, and the standoff drags. Her knees must hurt, but she stays. The house watches, torn between respect for her will and worry that this will harden into a fight. In a family where girls are meant to obey, a long kneel in the hallway is a loud thing to do. The servants step around her, and the masters pass without a word, because no one wants to be the one to lift a daughter off the floor. A silent girl on her knees is harder to answer than a shouting one, and the house learns that the easy way out is not there.
