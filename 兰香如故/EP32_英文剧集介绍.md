# 兰香如故 — The Fragrance of Orchids Remains
## Episode 32 · Told Like A Story

Let me tell you about the thirty-second episode, because this is the one where the new peace in the capital turns out to be no peace at all for the sister left behind, and where the marquis who resigned a throne to protect his wife turns his hand, at last, to protecting a sister he once could not reach. The episode opens on Lin Jinqi learning what the household long hid: that his eldest sister, Lin Xiulai, married to Lu Jian, has been beaten by her husband for years, and the Lu family, counting the Lin name bent by scandal, treats her not as a daughter-in-law but as a thing to be corrected with a strap. The season marks how far the marquis has come — the man who would not let the old master flog him now goes, without the court's leave, to teach Lu Jian what a blow costs when it falls on the wronged. He beats the brother-in-law as a brother should, and the city that feared his title learns the title still means a man will answer for a raised hand.

Back in the Shen family's old residence, Lanxiang keeps the capital roof the way she once kept a great house — with a steward's eye and a secret's caution. She turns the empty rooms to use and, beneath the tending, gathers the threads of the Shen family's old case, the wrong done to the line the capital erased, the clues she has carried since the river. The season notes the easing between the married pair: the bride who woke angry on a northern boat now shares the threshold with the man who drugged her, and the wall she built at the tea service leans a hand's width toward him. But she is not won. She still keeps her guard, the way a woman keeps a hand near a door she may need to open, because the man who took her choice once can take it again, and the pen name Shen Rugù is not the only thing she hides behind. The old residence holds, tonight, a wife who is softer than she was and warier than she seems.

The plea that pulls Lanxiang from her quiet gathering comes from the second daughter, who carries the news of the eldest sister's beating as a weight she cannot bear alone. She comes to the Shen residence and asks Lanxiang — not the marquis, but the bride who freed a maid and bent a game — to save the sister the family could not. The season shows how the household now turns, instinctively, to the woman it once resented: the second daughter knows that Lin Jinqi will strike Lu Jian, but only Lanxiang can strike him where it counts, in the city's talk and the Lu family's pride. Lanxiang listens with the stillness of a steward who has seen this shape before, and the asking lands on ground the bride has already prepared, because she who rescued Lü Jier from a capital house knows precisely how a woman is kept and how she may be gotten out. The sister's cause, brought to her door, becomes the cause she takes.

Lanxiang answers not with a plea to the Lu family but with a design. She sends men of her own to lie in wait for Lu Jian and give him the beating he has given his wife, a rough justice dealt in an alley rather than a courtroom, so that the brother-in-law learns the cost of a strap upon a Lin daughter. And then, clever as the season has made her, she spreads the word through the capital that it was Lin Jinqi himself who struck the blow to protect his sister — a rumor planted on purpose, because a marquis's name laid over a private vengeance turns a shame into a warning the Lu family cannot answer. The season watches the bride who was once property turn the city's talk like a wheel: the same whispers that once mocked a maid now arm the Lin name, and Lu Jian, beaten and branded by rumor, finds the scandal he dealt come back to him as a storm he cannot weather.

The rumor does its work. The Lu family, stung, comes to the Lin estate to demand satisfaction for the beating of their son, expecting a cringing household — and instead meets Lin Jinqi, who until that moment has not known the half of it. The marquis hears their complaint and, for the first time, the full truth of his sister's years of beatings, and the season shows his cold fury break the courtly calm: he does not apologize, he expels them. He turns the Lu father and son out of the residence with the same hand that once taught Lu Jian his lesson, and the family that counted on the Lin name's shame finds the name is not bent but bared, ready to defend its own. The wrong the household hid is now, by Lanxiang's design and the marquis's wrath, a public matter the Lu family brought upon itself, and the gate that should have shut on a beaten sister shuts instead on the men who beat her.

Then the two of them act as one. Lanxiang and Lin Jinqi, inside and out, lead their people straight to the Lu family's back courtyard, where Lin Xiulai is kept, and there they stage the final blow: packets of beast's blood are laid upon the sister so she appears grievously hurt, a false wound meant to frighten the Lu household into letting her go rather than face a charge of near-murder. The season marks the strange mercy of the trick — a feigned injury that spares a real one, a scare that frees a woman without a drop shed. The Lu family, confronted with a "dying" Lin daughter and the marquis's cold men at their gate, yields, and Lin Xiulai is carried out of the house that abused her and back to the Lin estate, the sister the family failed now reclaimed by the brother and the bride who would not let her stay.

At the Lin estate the imperial physician is called, and Lanxiang, who knows the value of a story told well, coaches the telling so that the sister's hurts are spoken of as graver than they are, lending the feigned injury the weight of a true one in the record. The season watches a mistress who once bent a fever's tale now bend a court's, so that the wound becomes the proof the sister needs to leave. And Lin Jinqi, with his brothers at his back, stands behind the eldest sister's wish to be free of Lu Jian — to take the separation the law allows a wronged wife, a heqili that returns her to her birth house with her name intact. The rescue that began with a brother's fist ends with a sister's release, and the household that once smoothed the beating over now signs the paper that ends it, because the bride who would not be conquered has taught it to protect its own.

So the episode turns on the same truth the tale has carried from the bathwater: a woman kept low by a house will be freed not by the house's pity but by another woman's wit and a man's willing hand. Lin Xiulai's years of beatings end not because the Lu family repented but because Lanxiang designed the rumor, struck the blow by proxy, and faked the wound that broke the gate — and because Lin Jinqi, told at last the truth, chose his sister over his pride. The season sets the married pair at the center of the rescue they built together, the rift between them eased by a hair and the guard still up, and notes that the bride who slapped a groom now saves a sister with a blood pack and a well-told lie. The capital that feared her name holds, tonight, a household a little more whole and a great deal more honest than the one that married her.

So we close the thirty-second episode the way a gate closes on the men who beat a woman — with Lu Jian bruised by rumor and by proxy, the Lu father and son cast out, Lin Xiulai carried home, and the heqili signed under a physician's eye. Lin Jinqi learned of his sister's years and answered with his wrath; Lanxiang turned the city's talk and faked a wound to spring the gate; the second daughter brought the plea and the brothers backed the freedom. Episode thirty-three will carry the bride from the Shen residence to the page, where the pen name Shen Rugù begins, in earnest, the work of washing a family's name clean in the mouths of common people. For now, remember only this: the sister was freed not by the law's mercy but by a bride's design and a marquis's rage, and the wall between the married pair, leaned but not fallen, holds a woman who saves others the way she is learning, slowly, to save herself.
