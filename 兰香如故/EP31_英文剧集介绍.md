# 兰香如故 — The Fragrance of Orchids Remains
## Episode 31 · Told Like A Story

Episode 31 picks up the fight for Lü Jí'ér where the money left off. Lanxiang goes to Zhao Xingtang's door again, this time not to talk price but to take the girl out for good. She stands in front of Zhao and says plainly that the bond is settled and Lü Jí'ér is to walk out with her. No more soft words, no more dance. She has the paper, she has the plan, and she means to leave that house with her friend. Zhao is not used to a servant girl who comes back to collect, and the room goes still as the two women face each other over one small life that both claim to hold. Lanxiang has played the soft game and the smart game already, and neither finished the job, so today she comes as the one who will not leave without her friend. Zhao reads the change in her face and knows this is not a girl who can be waved off with a smile. The papers in Lanxiang's sleeve are real, and the quiet in her voice says she has come to collect what is owed, not to ask for a favor from anyone in this room.

Zhao Xingtang does not give her up easily. She says the bond was one thing but the girl's service is another, and she tries to twist the matter so Lü Jí'ér stays under her roof a while longer. It is the old trick of a person with power, to agree in words and block in deed. Lanxiang sees it at once. She does not raise her voice. She simply lays the facts down, one by one, until Zhao has no clean ground to stand on. The two women circle each other in talk, and the longer it goes, the clearer it is that Lanxiang came ready and Zhao came expecting to brush her off, which she cannot. Zhao tries the old moves, delay, a half yes, a small task invented to keep the girl one more day, but Lanxiang meets each with a flat answer that closes the door Zhao opens. The fine ladies watching stop whispering, because they can see a servant out-think their host, and that is a sight none of them expected in this room. Zhao's power rests on people fearing her, and fear is exactly what Lanxiang has run out of, after all she has been through.

Just when the standoff could turn ugly, Lin Jinqi walks in. He has been close enough to hear, and he chooses this moment to step to Lanxiang's side. His wife Zhao Xingtang is surprised to see him back her opponent, but he is the master of the house and his word carries. He tells Zhao the matter is done, the girl is free, and there will be no more keeping her. His voice is flat and final. The surprise in the room is real, because the husband has just sided with the bride against his own wife, and everyone there knows what that means about where his loyalty now sits. For Zhao it is a public shame, her own husband taking the other side in her own house, and she has no way to save face without losing the point. Lin Jinqi does not enjoy the moment, but he does not waver either, because he has decided where the right lies and he will stand on it. The servants at the door lower their eyes, knowing they just watched the true order of the house show its face in front of them all.

With Lin Jinqi behind her, Lanxiang gets what she came for. Zhao steps back, the last door opens, and Lü Jí'ér is brought out. The rescue is not a fight with fists but a wall of will that finally breaks, helped by the one man Zhao cannot answer. Lanxiang takes her friend's hand and does not let go. The three of them, Lanxiang, Lin Jinqi, and Lü Jí'ér, walk out of that house together, and the girl who was property a minute before is now a person walking on her own feet, with the papers in Lanxiang's sleeve to prove it. No one stops them at the gate, because the master's word walked ahead of them like a key. Lü Jí'ér's steps are small at first, as if she expects the leash to snap back, then they steady as the wall falls behind. Lanxiang keeps her hand tight, not from fear but from want, the want to never let this friend slip into another's claim again. The three cross the threshold and the house that held her loses her for good, and the door shuts behind them.

Out in the open, Lü Jí'ér breathes free air for the first time in a long while. She is no longer Zhao's, no longer a name on a bond. The weight that sat on her shoulders for years lifts, and she looks at Lanxiang with eyes that finally hold light instead of fear. She does not weep and she does not make a scene. She simply stands straight, the way a person stands when the chain comes off. The capital that held her tight now lies behind her, and ahead is a road she gets to choose, a thing she has not had since she was small. She turns once to look at the roof that kept her, not with hate but with a plain kind of done, the way a person looks at a cage after the door is open and there is no reason to go back. Lanxiang watches her and feels the old knot in her own chest loosen a little, because saving someone you love is the one win that asks nothing back. The city air, which once felt like a weight, now feels like room to breathe, and she draws it in slow.

What Lü Jí'ér chooses next is her own. She decides to start a new life, not as a servant to anyone, but on her own footing. She will go to Quanzhou and set up a household headed by a woman, a home with no master, where she answers only to herself. It is a bold road for a girl raised to serve, and she knows it will be hard. But the hard part is hers to carry now, and that is the point. She thanks Lanxiang once, plain, and turns toward the south, a free person walking toward a free door, with the gold sewn into her clothes to help her begin. Lanxiang tells her the road south is long and the start will be thin, but she says it without pity, as one free woman to another. Lü Jí'ér nods, because she would rather be poor and her own than fed and owned. The two part with a look that needs no more words, the kind of look that only girls who survived the same house can share between them. Then Lü Jí'ér walks, and the south takes her, and the old life closes behind like a book put down.

After Lü Jí'ér is gone, Lanxiang and Lin Jinqi are left alone with the quiet that follows a hard win. They have just stood together against his own wife, and that changes the air between them. She does not thank him right away, because thanks between these two is a tangled thing, but she looks at him a moment longer than she used to. He reads it, or thinks he does, and for the first time since the boat he feels the wall between them crack just a little. The rescue they pulled off side by side opens a small space neither of them expected to find. They stand in the street a moment, not quite ready to go back to the Lin residence, because that house holds the wife Lin Jinqi just crossed. The quiet between them is not the cold quiet of before but a newer, unsure one, the kind that comes after two people pull the same rope. Lanxiang folds the papers and tucks them away, and the small act feels like closing a chapter neither of them speaks about yet, but both feel it close, like a door softly shut behind them.

Then Lin Jinqi says the thing he has held back. He tells Lanxiang, plain, that he does not want a wife who stays by force. He wants her, the real her, to choose to be his wife. The words come out quiet but full, because this is the honestest he has been since he carried her onto that boat. He admits the marriage began wrong, with a drugged cup and a locked door, and he says he would trade all of it for one true yes from her. It is not a pretty speech, just a man laying his want on the table, asking for something he cannot take. He does not dress it up as love poetry or grand promises. He says it the way he says most things, straight and plain, because he knows she would see through any pretty shell. The boat, the drug, the carried bride, he names each as his own wrong, and he does not ask her to forget them. What he asks instead is a chance, a slow one, to be chosen rather than owned, and the asking itself is the biggest turn he has made since the day they met on that forced road.

He goes further and names what is in his chest. He says he only ever wanted Lanxiang to be his wife in truth, not in name on a paper, not in a bed she was carried to. The force was his mistake, and he owns it now without excuse. What he asks for instead is her heart, if she can ever bring it, and he says he will wait rather than take. The confession is raw for a man of his standing, who is used to being obeyed, not begging. But he begs, in his own plain way, for the one thing no rank and no silver can buy him. To a man raised to command, this is a strange posture, standing open-handed before a girl he forced, but he holds it because the old way gave him nothing but a cold bed and a slapped face. He would rather have one honest glance from her than a thousand silent nights. The words hang in the air between them, and for once he does not fill the silence with another order, letting her be the one to answer or not, as she pleases.

So episode 31 turns the story from rescue to reckoning of the heart. Lanxiang faced Zhao and, with Lin Jinqi at her back, walked her friend out of a life as property. Lü Jí'ér takes the open road south to a home of her own making. And Lin Jinqi, the man who forced the marriage, finally says out loud that force was the wrong door, and the only one he wants opened is the one Lanxiang chooses to open. The wall between them is not down, but for the first time he has knocked on it instead of kicking it through, and she has heard the knock. What comes next is hers to decide, and he has finally left the door to her hand. The capital that broke her family also gave her this strange turn, a husband who learned to ask. Whether she can ever open what he forced shut is a question the next episodes will have to carry, but for tonight the wall has a knock on it, and on the other side, for the first time, a man who is actually waiting to be let in, without forcing the latch.
